EXTERNAL ASSESSMENT · 01

Paper 1

Approaches, concepts and contexts

1 hour 30 minutes 35 marks · 35% of SL grade

Section A · Short-answer questions

Two compulsory 4-mark questions focusing on the psychological approaches.

Section B · Application questions

Two compulsory 6-mark questions requiring you to apply psychological knowledge to an unfamiliar situation.

Section C · Extended response

One 15-mark concept-based extended response.

EXTERNAL ASSESSMENT · 02

Paper 2

Research methods and practical work

1 hour 30 minutes 35 marks · 35% of SL grade

Section A · Class practicals

Four compulsory questions based on the practical investigations completed during your course.

Section B · Unseen research study

Evaluate an unfamiliar research study using relevant psychological concepts.

What to prepare

Research aims, methods, procedures, ethics, sampling, operationalization and limitations.

ANSWER WRITING

Understand the command terms.

The command term tells you what kind of thinking the examiner expects.

01

Define

Give the precise meaning of a psychological term or concept.

Approach:

Provide a clear definition. Avoid unnecessary background information.

02

Explain

Make an idea understandable by showing how or why it occurs.

Approach:

Explain the mechanism, then develop it using relevant details.

03

Apply

Use psychological knowledge in a particular situation.

Approach:

Connect specific details from the scenario to the relevant theory.

04

Evaluate

Consider the strengths, limitations and implications of an idea.

Approach:

Explain why each strength or limitation affects the conclusion.

05

Discuss

Present a balanced and developed argument.

Approach:

Explore different perspectives and finish with a justified judgement.

THE THINKING FRAMEWORK

Six psychological concepts.

Connect these concepts to the psychological content you study. They should inform your thinking, not simply appear as memorised definitions.

01

Bias

Systematic influences that may distort research or interpretation.

02

Causality

Whether evidence supports a cause-and-effect relationship.

03

Change

How and why behaviour and mental processes transform over time.

04

Measurement

How psychological variables are defined and assessed.

05

Perspective

How different approaches explain human behaviour.

06

Responsibility

Ethical obligations when conducting and applying research.

THE PEONY STUDY METHOD

Don't just memorise.
Understand the why.

For every psychological concept, ask yourself: What is it? How does it work? Why does it happen? What evidence supports it? What are its limitations? How can I apply it to a real situation?
